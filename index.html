Method

A simple 3-step safety check

Digital Guardians walks you through each message calmly, so you always know what to look for and what to do next.

01

Understand what you received

  • Paste any email, text, or message you’re unsure about.
  • Include links, phone numbers, and names if you can.
  • Optionally add a short note about what worries you.

Result: A clear view of what the message is really asking you to do.

02

Spot the red flags

  • We highlight urgency, pressure, or strange requests.
  • You see which parts are risky and why.
  • We suggest safer options when something looks off.

Result: You know exactly which details should make you careful.

03

Act with confidence

  • Follow simple, step-by-step advice for what to do next.
  • Practice similar situations in the learning area.
  • Share the guidance with family or friends if needed.

Result: Fewer doubts, fewer scares, and more calm decisions online.

Our story

Born from real conversations with seniors

Digital Guardians grew out of simple questions seniors were already asking: “Is this email real?” “Can I trust this text?” “I don’t want to bother my family every time.”

Instead of more fear, we wanted to offer calm structure — a way to slow down, look closely at a message, and make a decision that feels safe and confident.

We listened to older adults, caregivers, and community leaders to design an experience that feels friendly, simple, and respectful. Every word is chosen to reduce pressure, not add it.

Today, Digital Guardians continues to evolve with real feedback, new scam patterns, and one clear goal: help more people enjoy the internet without constant worry.

If something has already happened

Think you’ve been scammed? Here’s what to do.

You’re not alone. Take a breath, follow these steps, and reach out to the right people — the sooner you act, the more you can protect.

What to do right now

  1. Don’t panic. These things happen to smart, careful people every day.
  2. Stop all contact with the person, number, or website.
  3. Call your bank using the number on the back of your card.
  4. Change passwords for any affected accounts.
  5. Write down what happened so you can explain it clearly when you report it.

Where to report it

  • FTC (USA): reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: antifraudcentre.ca
  • Local police: Use the non-emergency line in your area.

Questions you might have

What Digital Guardians is (and isn’t)

A clear companion for online safety — not a bank, not a legal service, and not a replacement for official fraud reporting.

Who is Digital Guardians for?

Primarily for seniors, their families, and community helpers who want a calm, structured way to check messages and build safer habits online.

What does it actually do?

It helps you review suspicious messages, spot common scam patterns, and choose safer next steps, using simple language and practical examples.

Does it replace my bank or the police?

No. Digital Guardians does not move money, block transactions, or investigate crimes. If you think you’ve been scammed, you should still contact your bank and local authorities.

Do I need an account or to pay?

You can use the checker and learning resources without creating an account. Messages are analyzed but not stored, and the experience is designed to be as simple and low-pressure as possible.
